Model creation and methods

Final Model 2 estimates county entry of raccoon rabies over 20 years after cessation of USDA oral rabies vaccination. It combines a fitted county habitat model, distance-based landscape spread, and annual human-mediated translocation along selected Interstates.

1. Study design and initial conditions

The modeled geographic domain contains the same 3,096 contiguous-US counties or county equivalents used in the accepted spread reconstruction. The initial endemic footprint is fixed at 877 counties. Model year zero is the start of the cessation scenario, rather than a specified calendar date. Connecticut was absent from the supplied spread-domain files and remains outside this domain. The initial footprint is retained independently of the counties used to fit the habitat model.

Natural movement is represented on a 10-km landscape graph. Human-mediated introductions can establish geographically separated nidi, which can initiate subsequent landscape spread and contribute future transport origins. A county is counted as reached when the first represented landscape cell is entered. This does not mean that every part of that county is infected.

2. Habitat model: eligibility, outcome, and inputs

County eligibility and the fixed P(free) outcome

The two supplied county files were paired only after all 3,292 row-by-row county names and normalized state labels matched. Five-digit county FIPS identifiers and rabies virus variant assignments came from the 2024 variant workbook. Counties were eligible if the variant field contained the whole token RAC, including mixed RAC and North Central skunk designations. An exact duplicate Baltimore-city record was collapsed, giving 891 unique eligible FIPS before vaccination exclusions.

USDA county bait-distribution records were joined by FIPS. Any positive number of baits in distribution years 2020–2024 excluded that county from habitat fitting. The distribution-year field, rather than fiscal year, was used. This removed 176 counties and retained 715: 702 RAC-only and 13 mixed RAC/skunk counties. A missing record means no positive USDA distribution record was found in this source; it does not prove the absence of every state, local, or unrecorded vaccination effort. Partial-county baiting was sufficient for exclusion.

The supplied county probability of terrestrial rabies freedom, P(free), was treated as a fixed input. Its derivation and accepted use were not changed. The forest response was:

County response = 1 − P(free)

A higher response therefore indicates a lower supplied probability of rabies freedom. The fitted forest prediction is the habitat suitability/receptivity score used downstream. It is not a separate per-introduction biological establishment probability.

Land cover: 11 predictors

County land-cover summaries were retrieved from the USGS/MRLC Land Cover Atlas service, with GeoId equal to the county FIPS, StartDate 2001, and EndDate 2021. The 2021 endDate class areas were used. Each class area was divided by the sum of all 16 reported class areas. The resulting fractions were combined into the following 11 predictors. These are county composition fractions, not raw county areas.

Latitude and human density: two predictors

Latitude was the 2020 Census Gazetteer internal-point latitude. Human population density was calculated from the April 1, 2020 population-estimates base in the Census 2020–2021 county population file, divided by 2020 Gazetteer land area in square kilometers. The model used the natural-log transformation ln(1 + people/km²). This transformation reduces the influence of extreme urban densities while retaining low-density variation.

Mapped sewershed coverage: one predictor

The 14th predictor was mapped wastewater service-area coverage from EPA's national sewershed product. The national snapshot contains 17,012 publicly owned wastewater-treatment service-area polygons, including externally sourced and EPA-modeled boundaries. Polygons were assigned by spatial overlap with counties, not by the county containing the treatment plant. Data pages were checked against the source object identifiers to detect missing downloads.

Sewershed coverage (%) = 100 × area[union of sewershed polygons intersecting the county footprint] / area[county footprint]

County footprints came from the 2020 Census 1:500,000 cartographic boundary file. County and sewer geometries were projected to the equal-area EPSG:5070 coordinate system. Invalid sewer geometries were repaired before intersection. Overlapping pieces were unioned before measuring area, preventing double counting. The fitting-cohort predictor values were retained exactly when extending prediction nationally; all 707 fitting counties overlapping the spread domain matched their fitting inputs. The other eight fitting counties are the historical Connecticut counties outside the spread domain.

The denominator is the cartographic county footprint, which approximates county land coverage but does not erase all inland water. A value of zero means no mapped polygon overlap; it is not verified absence of sewer service. This variable measures mapped service-area extent, not pipe length, stormwater-network density, or raccoon access to pipes. Regional connections and modeled boundary uncertainty can produce local inaccuracies. Source-quality flags were retained for audit rather than added as predictors. The county-error review identified likely underrepresentation of Falls Church's sewer network by the national layer; no replacement value was invented for that county.

3. Random Forest fitting and validation

Counties were sorted by five-digit FIPS. A fixed random sample of 72 counties, approximately 10%, was withheld using NumPy default_rng with seed 20261026; the remaining 643 counties were used for fitting and tuning. The county assignments were retained for all subsequent comparisons.

Five shuffled random cross-validation folds were created within the 643 training counties using seed 20261027. Candidate forests had 500 bootstrap trees, squared-error splitting, unlimited maximum depth, and random seed 20261026. The tuning grid combined max_features values of 0.5 or 1.0 with min_samples_leaf values of 2, 5, or 10. Selection minimized mean validation-fold root mean squared error (RMSE). The selected sewer-inclusive forest used max_features = 0.5 and min_samples_leaf = 2. Other estimator settings followed scikit-learn 1.8.0 defaults.

No holdout outcomes were used to fit a tree or select its tuning settings. The fixed holdout was, however, inspected during successive model-development comparisons. The following statistics are therefore development evaluation on a reused random holdout, rather than a new independent external validation.

The fitted model, exact fitting table, tuning results, holdout predictions, and software settings are included in the provenance folder.

The final forest predicts scores for all 3,096 spread-domain counties. Those predictions extend relationships learned in the fitting cohort to the national covariate range; some national counties have predictor values outside the fitting cohort's marginal ranges. The model was retained for parsimony; spending, climate, and elevation additions are not included in this final forest.

Habitat categories used for spread speed

Four fixed score intervals connect the forest to the landscape model. The exact historical numerical breakpoints were not archived; boundaries were reconstructed as the midpoint between the highest saved score in one category and the lowest in the next. These reproduce all 3,096 archived category assignments. The retained boundaries are 0.244868132, 0.509563106, and 0.728591675. Scores below the first boundary are no/very low, then low, moderate, and high as successive boundaries are crossed. They are fixed across scenarios; changing the nidus suitability cutoff does not reclassify habitat.

4. Landscape spread and water barriers

The existing EPSG:5070 landscape graph uses 10-km cells with horizontal, vertical, and diagonal neighbors. Each node inherits its county's habitat category. Defaults are 0, 10, 25, and 50 miles/year for no/very low, low, moderate, and high habitat. A speed of zero blocks natural movement through that category. Positive speeds activate its available dry cells. Diagonal movement requires the adjacent orthogonal cells to be traversable, preventing movement across a blocked corner.

Travel time on an edge is its effective distance multiplied by the average inverse speed at its two endpoints. Horizontal and vertical distances are 10 km, and diagonal distances are 10√2 km before conversion to miles. Natural Earth major river crossings apply a fourfold travel-time penalty. Lakes with area at least 100 km² block the relevant cells or crossings, with the retained mapped Sandusky Bay bridge connection handled explicitly. River and lake rules are fixed in the interface.

Shortest-time paths from the same 877 year-zero counties produce the natural-only arrival baseline. For a county containing represented active nodes, its arrival is the earliest node entry. Small counties without a represented active center use the retained nearest-active-cell proxy. A nidus uses its county's representative origin node; onward travel times are recomputed with the selected speeds. The graph topology and water costs are the accepted reconstruction, not a recovered historical propagation matrix. Every displayed scenario recalculates from year zero with the final habitat categories.

5. Interstate routing and annual transport burden

Origins, destinations, and distances

Routing is fixed to the reconstructed 2024 Census TIGER primary-road network, retaining Interstate routes with even one- or two-digit mainline numbers. A destination is eligible only if its county centroid longitude is strictly west of the origin county's centroid longitude. Local bends in a route are allowed. Road distances are shortest-path projected network lengths in EPSG:5070, converted to miles; they are not navigator travel times. The saved routing network intersects 827 model counties.

Within the eligible origin set, origin weights are proportional to intersecting Interstate miles. Destinations within a sampled distance band are also weighted by their intersecting Interstate miles. The default short-haul share is 40% and the long-haul share is 60%. Short bands are 0–100, 100–250, 250–500, and 500–750 miles, each weighted 0.25. Long bands are 750–1,000, 1,000–1,500, 1,500–2,000, and 2,000–3,000 miles, weighted 0.25, 0.30, 0.25, and 0.20. An empty band uses the retained nearest populated band within the corresponding short/long class.

Long-haul origins are filtered for long-haul capability. Under the retained short-haul policy, 25 road counties have no eligible westbound short destination. A short-haul draw from one of these origins is unassigned. The national inferred transport mean and the mean assigned to modeled destinations are therefore reported separately in the annual export. No renormalization of this existing policy was introduced.

Growth in transport risk as counties become reached

The calibration is one detected transported rabid raccoon every five years (0.2/year), with detection probability 1%. The initial inferred mean is 0.2 ÷ 0.01 = 20 infectious transports/year. The burden-scaled default is:

Transport mean in year y = (year-zero detected mean ÷ detection probability) × Ny−1 / 877

Ny−1 is the number of counties reached at the start of year y, including counties reached naturally and through earlier nidi and their onward spread. Each county is counted once. This is a simple burden proxy that assumes unchanged average rabies burden per reached county. It is not a direct forecast of annual rabid raccoon cases or a population-weighted burden estimate.

At the start of each year, the model scales the national transport mean and updates eligible road origins from that same reached set. Newly established nidi contribute origins beginning the next year. The rate is scaled once by county count; normalized Interstate-mile weights allocate that total across origins and do not add another burden multiplier. The two transport-growth checkboxes can disable rate growth or origin feedback for scenario comparison. Both are enabled by default.

Annual Monte Carlo calculation

For each county and year, the inferred national mean is multiplied by its combined short/long destination weight to obtain annual arrival intensity λ. Given the shared scenario state, the number of new receipts follows Poisson thinning. The probability of at least one receipt in that year is:

q = 1 − exp(−λ)

The 10,000 histories track whether each county has ever received a rabid raccoon. If H histories have a previous receipt, the number newly receiving one is drawn as Binomial(10,000 − H, q). Adding this count to H gives the cumulative receipt fraction H/10,000 at that year's end. This avoids storing every individual route event.

The histories sample deliveries conditional on one shared scenario frontier and ensemble-level nidus decisions. They are not 10,000 separate epidemic-front realizations. Future annual intensities change in response to that shared reached footprint. The optional direct Poisson mode replaces sampled fractions with 1 − exp(−cumulative λ), while retaining the same forward feedback rules. A fixed seed makes the Monte Carlo run reproducible; the default is 20261009.

6. The two nidus thresholds and annual event order

Threshold 1: sufficient evidence of a delivery. The cumulative probability that at least one rabid raccoon has arrived in a county must be strictly greater than the selected delivery cutoff. At the default 50%, more than 5,000 of the 10,000 histories must have received a delivery. This is cumulative through that year, not a 50% annual chance.

Threshold 2: sufficiently suitable habitat. The county's fixed forest score must be strictly greater than the selected suitability cutoff. The default, 0.7928484500378316, is the median updated score among the same 877 year-zero endemic counties. This screen is separate from delivery probability; the score is not multiplied by the number of deliveries and is not sampled as a per-animal establishment probability.

Both screens must pass. The county must be in the road-destination domain and must not already be reached by an existing natural or nidus-derived front by the end of that year. The county becomes a nidus in the first qualifying year; the model does not add a separate 5% establishment step. Its county entry is assigned to the establishment year. Onward landscape movement begins the following year. All years use the same order: start-of-year burden and origins; annual receipts; cumulative thresholds and front preemption; nidus establishment; then future onward arrival forecasts. This prevents a newly established nidus from increasing transport risk in the same year.

7. Map colors, outputs, and the default scenario

The map-year slider shows years zero through the selected horizon. Blue identifies the year-zero endemic footprint. Gold identifies subsequent arrival first from the main enzootic front. Coral identifies earlier arrival from a translocation nidus, including counties that the main front would reach later. Gray identifies counties not reached by the displayed year. Black stars mark independent nidi established by that year.

Within each hue, the final forest's model-predicted 1 − P(free) score sets a linear white-to-color shade: zero is white and one is the full hue. Higher values are darker. The same scale applies to every hue, including gray; gray shading describes the habitat-model score, not a year-specific presence probability. This follows the color-plus-probability shading approach of JAVMA annual surveillance Figure 1 without introducing a new freedom threshold or exclusion. The current scenario does not refit the forest or change these scores.

With all accepted defaults, year 20 reaches 2,006 counties: 877 initially endemic, 815 first reached from the main front, and 314 first reached from nidi. The natural-only baseline reaches 1,917. Of the 314 nidus-first counties, 225 would also be reached naturally by year 20, while 89 are additional to that baseline. The distinction between earlier arrival and additional year-20 reach is retained in the map and exports.

County CSV includes arrival times, earliest source at the map year, both cumulative delivery probability and the habitat score, and additional reach relative to natural-only spread. Annual CSV records the county burden multiplier, inferred and assigned transport means, origin counts, new nidi, and reached counts. Scenario JSON records all inputs and annual receipt histories. The SVG download preserves the accepted source hues, continuous shading, legend, map year, and nidus markers.

Expert input submissions are stored separately from active scenario controls. They never automatically overwrite the model. The administrator can export submissions as CSV. Institutional HTTPS, authentication, and persistent storage should be supplied when hosting beyond a local computer.
