FINAL MODEL 2 · METHODS

How the model was built

This model examines how raccoon rabies could spread if USDA oral rabies vaccination ceased, with no additional vaccination intervention represented. It combines an existing landscape-spread baseline with possible long-distance transport of infectious raccoons. Counties summarize the results; natural spread is calculated across a landscape grid.

1. Study design and timing

The model follows spread for up to 20 years from a defined starting distribution. Year 0 is the model start, rather than a particular calendar year. Two pathways are represented: gradual movement of an existing rabies front through suitable habitat, and transport of infectious raccoons to counties farther west. The interface reports arrival years, new independent foci, and counties reached beyond the baseline. It does not directly estimate infected-animal counts, human exposures, deaths, or economic costs.

The baseline is the archived county arrival surface supplied with the project. In archived-reference mode, the saved baseline and default speeds are preserved. In recalculated-landscape mode, the baseline, onward spread, and transport-origin timing are regenerated using the selected category speeds. The geographic network and water rules are fixed, but edge travel times and shortest paths are recalculated.

2. Derivation of habitat suitability scores

The habitat component was developed before the translocation scenarios. Its purpose was to estimate how favorable each county is for raccoon-rabies persistence, including counties beyond the observed endemic area. The original project calls this quantity habitat receptivity; the interface also uses the more familiar term habitat suitability. Both refer to the same saved score.

2.1 Surveillance-based outcome

According to the original methods summary [4], the model outcome was derived from the 2024 county probability-of-freedom estimates. Those estimates used multiple years of National Rabies Surveillance System testing and detection data to characterize the probability that a county was free of rabies. For each county with suitable source data, the habitat-model target was:

Training target = 1 − P(free of rabies)

For example, a county with P(free) = 0.20 would have a target of 0.80. This transformation reverses the direction of the freedom score: counties less likely to be free receive higher target values. It does not directly measure habitat quality, the probability that one infected animal will transmit, or the speed of spread. The suitability model uses environmental and geographic predictors to estimate this surveillance-derived target.

2.2 Environmental and demographic predictors

The Random Forest used 13 predictors: 11 National Land Cover Database land-cover variables, latitude, and the natural logarithm of human population density plus one. Land cover describes the landscape available to raccoons; latitude represents broad geographic gradients; transformed human density allows the relationship with settlement intensity to vary without letting a few densely populated counties dominate its numeric scale. The specified transformation is ln(density + 1), which is defined when density is zero. These statements describe the predictor roles, rather than proving that any predictor causes the observed rabies pattern.

Predictors = {11 land-cover variables, latitude, ln(human density + 1)}

A Random Forest combines predictions from many decision trees. Trees divide counties into groups according to combinations of predictors; the combined prediction can represent nonlinear relationships and interactions. For example, the association between a land-cover variable and rabies receptivity can depend on other landscape or geographic characteristics. The original summary identifies a Random Forest, but does not preserve its tree count, variable-sampling settings, minimum node size, or other fitting options. Those settings are not replaced with assumed software defaults here.

2.3 Development, holdout evaluation, and prediction

The development dataset contained 974 counties with suitable observed data. A fixed 10% holdout sample was reserved for evaluation. In this design, a holdout county is withheld during fitting and then predicted to assess performance on data not used to train the model. The remaining development data support fitting. The archived summary does not specify the holdout counties, selection seed, spatial separation, tuning process, or numerical evaluation results; therefore, no R², error statistic, calibration claim, or spatial-validation result is reported in this website.

The fitted relationship was applied to 3,096 county records to produce the saved predicted_receptivity values. The present website imports those predictions; it does not refit the forest when a user changes a scenario. The supplied domain includes 877 year-zero endemic counties but omits Connecticut. Prediction into counties outside the development sample is an extrapolation of the fitted relationship, not direct evidence that rabies would establish there.

2.4 Scores, categories, and their separate uses

The continuous suitability score is used to decide whether a potential translocation focus has sufficiently favorable habitat. The score is also accompanied by an original four-category classification that determines natural spread speed. These are two separate uses of the habitat model. Changing the suitability cutoff changes which new foci qualify. Changing category speeds changes travel through the landscape. Neither operation changes a county’s underlying score or its imported category.

2.5 Interpretation and remaining documentation

A predicted score of 0.70 must not be read as a measured 70% chance that a delivered infectious raccoon will start an outbreak. The score predicts a target derived from rabies freedom estimates, which reflect surveillance and disease history as well as the environmental relationships captured by the forest. Latitude and human density can also carry geographic and surveillance-related patterns. The model uses this score as an ecological suitability index; it has not been calibrated as a per-delivery establishment probability.

The recovered summary supports the outcome, predictor groups, sample size, holdout fraction, and prediction domain described above. Exact reproduction of the original forest still requires the 974-county training table, the 3,096-county predictor table, the precise land-cover variables and data vintage, preprocessing and exclusion rules, fitted forest or complete settings, split seed, performance results, and original category-cutoff rule. The website preserves this distinction between documented methods and missing numerical reproduction records.

3. Interstate transport network

The reconstructed network uses the US Census Bureau’s 2024 TIGER/Line national primary-roads data [1]. It retains Interstate records with an even, one- or two-digit route number. Both the origin and destination must be counties crossed by an eligible Interstate; there is no additional road-access buffer. The resulting network includes 827 eligible counties.

Road geometry is projected in meters using the US Albers equal-area coordinate system (EPSG:5070). Lines are simplified with a 75-meter tolerance, nearby vertices are snapped at 60 meters, and 20 synthetic links of no more than 2 km join selected nearby endpoints. These links approximate missing interchange or road connections. Shortest-path distances are calculated along the resulting undirected graph. This is a routing proxy; it does not reproduce truck navigation, interchange restrictions, traffic flows, or actual shipment records.

Trips must have a destination county centroid strictly west of the origin county centroid. A route may bend north, south, or east locally, but its net county-to-county displacement is westward. Origin and destination weights are proportional to eligible Interstate mileage within each county. A county with more eligible Interstate mileage therefore receives a larger sampling weight. This weighting is an assumption, not an estimate fitted to observed raccoon movements.

At the start of year y, potential origins are counties reached by the active baseline by the end of year y−1. This is the archived baseline in reference mode and the regenerated landscape baseline in recalculated mode. Long-haul origins must also have an eligible destination at least 750 routed miles away. New translocation foci do not become transport origins. Reference mode uses the saved annual destination probabilities. Recalculated mode reweights the saved per-origin destination kernels each year according to the new baseline arrival times; it retains the same origin and destination mileage weights, distance bands, and fallback rules.

If no band in that class can be routed, that event is lost. Annual destination probabilities are not rescaled to replace these lost events. Distance-band weights and fallback rules are reconstruction choices.

4. Infectious transport rate and annual Monte Carlo

The event-rate input refers to the direct detection of a transported rabid raccoon. It does not refer to a secondary case, an outbreak cluster, or every raccoon transported regardless of infection. The model infers the mean number of infectious transports per year from:

λ = d / c

Here, d is directly detected events per year and c is the chance that an infectious transport is detected and recognized as transported. The default values are d = 0.2 and c = 0.01, equivalent to one detected transport every five years and a conditional mean of 20 infectious transports per year. Detection probability is an assumed input, not a measured ascertainment rate. If the observed events are secondary infections rather than direct diagnoses of transported raccoons, this conversion would require revision.

The transport process is Poisson: events vary randomly around the annual mean, and counts in separate years are independent. Each event is allocated using the route and distance weights above. For county i in year y, the expected receipts are:

μyi = λ [(1 − h)qSyi + hqLyi]

h is the long-haul share. The saved arrays qS and qL give each county’s destination probability per potential short- or long-haul event for that year. Their sums can be below one because some trips cannot be routed.

The default calculation estimates cumulative receipt probability from 10,000 simulated histories, updating it after each year. An efficient calculation tracks the number of histories in which a county has received at least one infectious raccoon. Among histories with no previous delivery, the chance of a first receipt this year is 1 − exp(−μyi). New first-receipt histories are drawn as:

ΔHyi ∼ Binomial(10,000 − Hy−1,i, 1 − exp(−μyi))
p̂yi = Hyi / 10,000

H is the number of histories with at least one receipt. Under independent Poisson events and fixed route weights, this count update has the same probability distribution as explicitly simulating deliveries in all histories. It avoids storing millions of individual events. Repeated deliveries matter for whether the first receipt has occurred, but the current seeding rule does not require two or more deliveries. A fixed random seed makes a run reproducible with the same software and inputs.

The optional “Direct Poisson probability” mode replaces the sampled fraction with the exact value pyi = 1 − exp(−Σt=1…y μti). Both modes check counties annually. Monte Carlo sampling can shift a county’s threshold-crossing year near the cutoff; the direct mode removes that sampling variation. The 10,000 histories are used to estimate probability and are not added together as 10,000 times the animal movements in one scenario.

5. The two thresholds for a new focus

A county must pass two separate screens at the end of a model year. The first concerns whether an infectious raccoon has arrived; the second concerns the county’s suitability. Both use strictly greater-than comparisons.

ScreenQuestion it answersDefaultWhat it does not mean
1. Delivery-probability cutoffIs the cumulative chance of at least one infectious delivery high enough by this year?More than 50%, or more than 5,000 of 10,000 Monte Carlo historiesNot a 50% annual arrival chance and not the chance an arrival establishes
2. Habitat-suitability cutoffDoes this county’s predicted suitability exceed the selected score?Score greater than 0.7050952593606139Not a 70.5% biological establishment probability and not a change to the county’s spread-speed category

Delivery probability is cumulative from year 1 through the year being evaluated. If it reaches 60% in year 10, that means 60% of histories include at least one delivery somewhere in those ten years. Repeated deliveries within one history count once for this screen. Exactly 50% does not exceed a 50% cutoff.

The habitat score is fixed for a county. Its default cutoff is the year-zero endemic-county median described in Section 2. At the default settings, a county with delivery probability 60% and habitat score 0.75 passes both screens. A county with 60% and 0.65 fails the habitat screen; a county with 45% and 0.80 fails the delivery screen. The two values are not multiplied together.

A candidate passing both screens becomes an independent focus only if neither the active baseline nor an earlier translocation front has reached the county by that year, and it is an eligible transport county. Once reached, it cannot start another independent focus. Candidates qualifying in the same year are evaluated together. The focus county is marked in that year; outward natural spread begins the following year.

This is a probability-based scenario rule, not a draw of biological establishment after each actual delivery. There is no additional 5% establishment chance. A “new focus” is a designation under the combined screens and preemption rule, rather than an independently measured probability that local transmission will persist.

6. Natural spread and water barriers

The reconstructed spread surface is a 10-km grid with eight neighboring directions in EPSG:5070. Each cell inherits its county’s habitat-class speed, with all four speeds editable in the tool. Travel time accumulates along every grid step, using the step distance multiplied by the average of the two cells’ reciprocal speeds. A diagonal step is longer than a horizontal or vertical step. A category assigned zero speed is excluded from natural movement; at defaults, this is the no/very-low category. The prepared graph also includes dry cells in that category, so assigning a positive speed activates their connections. A zero-speed category can still receive a designated translocation focus if the two focus screens pass, but it cannot generate outward natural spread from its own cells. Diagonal moves cannot cut across blocked corners.

Water features come from Natural Earth’s 1:10 million rivers and lakes, version 5.0.0 [2,3]. Selected river features have scale rank ≤6. A grid edge crossing a selected river takes four times its ordinary travel time; rivers cause delay rather than an absolute stop. Lakes with projected area ≥100 km² block affected cells and crossing edges. Wetlands are not independently blocked. The coarse water layers are approximations of ecological barriers.

One explicit local connection restores the State Route 2 bridge and approaches across Sandusky Bay between Erie and Ottawa counties, Ohio. It follows approximately 37.20 km of mapped 2024 Census road geometry and accrues about 2.31 years at the local low-habitat speed of 10 miles per year. This permits a physical shoreline connection without opening Lake Erie generally or accelerating natural spread to road-traffic speed.

For each transport county, a focus begins at the traversable grid cell nearest its centroid. County arrival is the first reached cell within that county. At default speeds, fourteen small or otherwise unrepresented counties use the nearest traversable-cell proxy because they have no assigned grid center. Active cells and proxies are reevaluated when speeds change. These rules summarize arrival at county scale while retaining distance-based travel through large counties; first arrival does not instantly infect the entire county.

In reference mode, the saved matrix provides travel time Dsi from a focus in county s to county i. If the focus is designated in year ts, its outward arrival is ts + 1 + Dsi. Each county keeps the earliest of its baseline arrival and arrivals from all retained foci. The focus county itself is marked at ts.

6.1 What happens when a spread speed changes

Changing any category speed selects “Recalculate from year 0.” The model reweights each grid edge using the speeds at its endpoints, applies the unchanged river multiplier and lake mask, and finds new shortest-time paths. Thus, paths may change as well as travel times. The baseline starts with the same 877 endemic counties; all active grid cells within those counties are starting cells. The model then updates which Interstate-origin counties are reached at the start of each year, recomputes delivery probabilities, reapplies the two focus screens, and propagates any retained foci on the same reweighted graph.

To make a speed comparison, select recalculated mode for both the default-speed run and the alternative-speed run. The recalculated default-speed baseline reaches 1,373 counties by year 20, whereas the archived baseline reaches 1,358. This difference comes from reconstruction of the original surface, not from changing a speed. Reference mode retains the agreed Final Model 2 result; choosing it restores the default speeds. Landscape mode and all four speeds are stored in the exported scenario.

7. Regeneration, outputs, and scenario comparison

Changing a scenario input triggers a new annual calculation and refreshes the map automatically. Live controls are detected-event frequency, detection probability, long-haul share, receipt cutoff, habitat cutoff, all four category-specific spread speeds, and the 1–20-year horizon. Calculation mode and random seed are also selectable. Distance bands, route scope, predicted scores, habitat-category assignments, water rules, and new-source feedback remain fixed. Speeds are applied to the saved grid topology rather than held fixed in a precomputed travel-time matrix. The expert-input form can collect proposals for these assumptions, but submitting an estimate does not change the active scenario.

At any map year, colors distinguish counties reached by the baseline from counties reached only because of translocation; independent foci have their own color. The annual CSV gives baseline and scenario county counts and the difference. The county CSV gives identifiers, arrival times, cumulative receipt probability at the scenario horizon, habitat score, and focus year. Scenario JSON records the inputs and results, including the random seed. The map downloads as an SVG. Fractional arrival times are preserved for year-boundary comparisons.

For paired scenarios, keep the random seed fixed to reduce incidental Monte Carlo changes. A seed does not supply uncertainty bounds for biological assumptions. To assess assumption uncertainty, run defined alternative parameter sets and report the resulting range separately from Monte Carlo sampling variation.

8. Reproduction checks and limitations

With the packaged default inputs and random seed 20261009, the website identifies two independent foci: Pulaski County, Arkansas in year 11 and Juneau County, Wisconsin in year 14. By year 20, 1,522 counties are reached, compared with 1,358 in the archived baseline, for 164 additional counties. The direct Poisson mode yields the same two foci and count difference. These are regression checks for this implementation, rather than independent validation of its assumptions.

The original landscape travel-time matrix was unavailable. The current matrix was reconstructed from county habitat classes and the water rules above. In a separate diagnostic, four archived western foci added 37 counties using this reconstruction, compared with 135 in the archived accepted analysis. This mismatch remains unresolved. Reference mode retains the archived baseline and uses reconstructed onward travel times; recalculated mode uses the reconstructed graph for both; agreement with the default scenario count does not establish geographic validity.

Other limitations include incomplete habitat-score provenance; the omitted Connecticut records; uniform speeds within each county; coarse grid and water geometry; assumed transport, detection, and distance weights; synthetic road links; fixed annual transport intensity; and no feedback from new foci to transport origins. There is no within-county transmission model, explicit host density or infection prevalence, stochastic local fadeout, seasonality, reintroduction biology, or changing management response. These assumptions define the scenario’s scope and should accompany any published map or impact estimate.
